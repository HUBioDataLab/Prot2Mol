# Does the reward model's ligand ranking depend on the protein?

Measured on eight trained checkpoints spanning four points in training, on both
data splits. Generated by `run_seen_unseen_grid.py` + `make_grid_report.py`.

## Summary

On molecules the model has already seen, knowing the correct protein is worth up to
**+0.42 Spearman** — about half of the model's entire ranking ability. On molecules
with scaffolds held out of training, the same knowledge is worth **+0.00 to +0.03**,
with confidence intervals spanning zero at every epoch.

The model does learn to use the protein. What it learns does not survive a new scaffold.

## Method

For each model, ranking-eligible assays (>= 3 ligands, >= 0.5 pChEMBL span) are drawn
from both the training file and the validation file of the split that model was
trained on. Each assay's ligands are then re-scored under four protein conditions,
and Spearman is computed against that assay's own measured pChEMBL ordering.

| axis | levels |
|---|---|
| molecules | `train` assays = seen during training · `val` assays = held out |
| protein | `true` · `other_train` (different protein, from train) · `other_val` (different protein, from val) · `novel` (BIOSNAP protein absent from **both** ChEMBL training sets) |

**Metric.** Weighted Spearman: each assay contributes in proportion to its ligand
count, matching `eval_assay_spearman.jsonl` written during training. Macro
(unweighted) is given alongside, since the two differ by ~0.04 on this data.

### Pipeline validation

Scoring was verified against the training logs before any conclusions were drawn.
Re-scoring every eligible assay of the scaffold validation split with
`checkpoint-5916` reproduces that run's own logged epoch-3 numbers exactly:

| | weighted | macro | eligible assays |
|---|---|---|---|
| this pipeline | 0.1831 | 0.1602 | 3969 |
| the run's own log | 0.1831 | 0.1604 | 3969 |

### A degenerate cell, and why `novel` exists

Both splits hold out molecules and assays, never targets:

| split | val targets | val-only targets | val molecules seen in train |
|---|---|---|---|
| scaffold | 1721 | **0** | 0.0% |
| random | 2380 | **0** | **61.5%** |

So a protein taken from the validation file is still a protein the model trained on,
and `other_val` is not an unseen-protein condition. It is reported anyway — it tracks
`other_train` closely, which is the evidence for that claim. The `novel` arm (758
BIOSNAP proteins absent from both training sets) supplies the genuinely unseen case.

## Reading

1. **Seen molecules: protein dependence grows steadily with training.** Scaffold split
   goes +0.01 (ep3) to +0.42 (ep100); random split +0.00 to +0.40. The architecture and
   the objective are perfectly capable of learning target conditioning.
2. **Unseen molecules, scaffold split: nothing transfers.** Every epoch has a confidence
   interval spanning zero, and two of the four point estimates are negative. A model
   that gains +0.42 from the protein on training molecules gains nothing on new scaffolds.
3. **The random split overstates the case.** It retains ~+0.20 on 'held-out' molecules,
   but 61.5% of those molecules occur in training. Compare the two splits at epoch 100:
   +0.20 when the molecules are mostly memorized, +0.03 when they are genuinely new.
4. **Any wrong protein costs about the same.** `other_train`, `other_val` and `novel`
   land within a few points of each other. A protein from a different database is no
   worse than one from the training set, which is what you expect if the model keys on
   target identity rather than reading sequence content.
5. **Training degrades held-out ranking.** Scaffold validation goes 0.102 (ep3) to 0.058
   (ep12, ep32) while training ranking climbs to 0.863. The best held-out checkpoint is
   the least-trained one.

## Limitations

- **100 assays per cell.** Per-assay Spearman is noisy; the 95% sampling range for a
  100-assay mean on this data is roughly +-0.09. Cell-level absolute values should be read
  with that in mind. The protein effect is a *paired within-assay* difference, so it is
  substantially more stable than the absolute values — but it is the same 100 assays.
- **Assays capped at 64 ligands** for this run, which excludes ~10% of assays. Those are
  the largest and highest-weighted ones, so absolute values here run below a full-split
  evaluation (0.159 vs 0.201 macro for `scaffold_ep3` on the test split).
- **Epoch bands are not perfectly matched across splits** (scaffold ep3 vs random ep1;
  ep12 vs ep10), and the early/mid bands mix architectures, because `save_total_limit: 2`
  left only two checkpoints per run. Prior ablations moved scaffold Spearman by <0.01
  across architectures, so this is unlikely to matter, but the comparison is not controlled.
- **`other_val` is not an unseen protein** (see above). Only `novel` is.
