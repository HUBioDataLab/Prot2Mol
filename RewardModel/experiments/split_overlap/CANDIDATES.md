# Candidate protein groups for the cross-protein ranking test

Generated by `find_shared_molecule_groups.py` + `make_candidates_report.py`.

## The test these candidates enable

Take a molecule set that TWO different proteins have each really been measured
against, where the two measured orderings disagree. Give the model the set with
protein A, then the identical set with protein B. The molecules are fixed, so a
protein-blind model can only emit one ordering and must fail at least one of the
two ground truths. A target-conditioned model should track whichever protein it
was given.

This is strictly sharper than a decoy-protein swap: both proteins are real, both
orderings are real, and the failure mode is directly observable rather than
inferred from a correlation drop.

## Selection criteria

| criterion | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| different targets | required | otherwise there is nothing to discriminate |
| same split part | required | A and B must both be train, or both val |
| shared molecules | >= 6 | your floor; most candidates have far more |
| pChEMBL span, each protein | >= 1.0 | each protein needs a real ordering, not noise |
| `gt_spearman` | <= 0.2 | **the key filter** — the two ground truths must disagree |

`gt_spearman` is Spearman between the two proteins' measured orderings of the
shared molecules. At +1 the proteins rank identically and the test cannot
discriminate no matter how many molecules are shared. Near 0 or negative is what
makes the experiment decisive.

## Caveats

- `gt_spearman` is computed on **median-aggregated** pChEMBL per (assay, molecule),
  so it inherits that aggregation. Measurement noise alone is ~0.25 log units, so a
  disagreement smaller than the span is not necessarily biological.
- Some very large shared sets (hundreds to 10,163 molecules) come from screening
  campaigns (Potency/qHTS) run against several targets. They are legitimate but
  noisier per molecule; the 6-40 band avoids them.
- Two targets sharing molecules are often homologues (same family, panel-screened).
  That is what makes the test fair rather than trivial: a model keying on gross
  sequence features will not separate them, which is the point.
- All candidates here are **within one split part**, as required.


## Is the ground-truth disagreement large enough? (added after review)

The three large groups highlighted above were selected by **molecule-set size**, not
by ranking disagreement, so they are not automatically good candidates. Measured
pairwise `gt_spearman` over each group's shared set:

| group | pairs | median `gt_rho` | range | ceiling on signal (1 - rho) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| scaffold val, 4 targets x 273 mols | 6 | **+0.381** | +0.323 .. +0.666 | 0.33 - 0.68 | moderate |
| random val, 4 targets x 70 mols | 6 | **+0.279** | +0.143 .. +0.651 | 0.35 - 0.86 | best of the three |
| scaffold train, 3 targets x 501 mols | 3 | **+0.694** | +0.592 .. +0.779 | 0.22 - 0.41 | **too similar — avoid** |

### Why `gt_rho` sets the ceiling

Let `S_A`, `S_B` be the model's predicted orderings given protein A and protein B on
the same molecules. Define

    delta = mean[ rho(S_A,GT_A), rho(S_B,GT_B) ] - mean[ rho(S_A,GT_B), rho(S_B,GT_A) ]
            \________ matched _________/         \_______ mismatched ________/

For a perfectly protein-blind model `S_A == S_B`, so the two means are built from the
same four terms and **delta is exactly 0** — independent of how good the model is and
independent of `gt_rho`. That is a clean null hypothesis.

For a perfectly target-conditioned model, `delta = 1 - gt_rho`. So **1 - gt_rho is the
ceiling on the observable signal**, which is what makes the scaffold-train group
(ceiling 0.22-0.41) a poor choice and the strongly-disagreeing pairs below a good one.

Note also that a label-free primary metric exists and does **not** depend on `gt_rho` at
all: `rho(S_A, S_B)`, the agreement between the model's own two predictions. A blind
model scores exactly 1.0 there whatever the ground truths do. `gt_rho` matters only for
the secondary question of whether the model tracks the *correct* ground truth.

Attenuation runs the wrong way for the weak group: cross-target orderings have
split-half reliability ~0.89, so an observed `gt_rho` of +0.694 implies a true value
near +0.78. Those three proteins genuinely rank these molecules alike.

### Suggested design

Run **two tiers**:

1. **Strong-disagreement tier** — the pairs above. Maximum power; if the model is
   target-conditioned at all, `delta > 0` should show here.
2. **Unbiased sweep** — sample pairs across the whole `gt_rho` range and plot `delta`
   against `1 - gt_rho`. A target-conditioned model gives a positive slope; a blind
   model gives a flat line at zero. That is far more convincing than a single tier,
   because it predicts the *shape* of the result rather than one number.

| random / train | 1,868 | 276 | 167 | 72 | **31** | -0.747 |
| random / val | 86 | 12 | 4 | 0 | **0** | -0.152 |

**Availability is very uneven across cells.** The two training cells are rich: 48 and 31
distinct target pairs reach `rho <= -0.3`, down to -0.747. `scaffold / val` is thin but
workable (2 pairs at <= -0.3). **`random / val` has none at all below -0.2** — its most
negative pair over >= 30 molecules is only -0.152.

That matters, because the validation cells are the scientifically interesting ones
(unseen molecules) and they are exactly where strong disagreement is scarce.

### Two points on picking between them

**Large n compensates for modest rho.** TLR8/TLR9 has only `rho = -0.14`, but with 742-783
shared molecules the per-assay Spearman is estimated very precisely, and the ceiling on
the signal (`1 - rho`) is still 1.14. A 36-molecule pair at -0.41 has a higher ceiling
(1.41) but a much noisier estimate. Running both covers the trade-off.

**TLR8/TLR9 appears in both validation cells**, with nearly the same molecule set
(742 vs 783). The identical comparison can be run in scaffold/val and random/val, which
isolates the effect of molecule leakage while holding the protein pair fixed. That is
the single most useful candidate in the list.
